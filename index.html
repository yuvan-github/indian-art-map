Exploring the Geographic Spread and Influence of Indian Art Styles

India’s artistic heritage did not grow in one place. It developed region by region — shaped by religion, royal courts, temples and monasteries, local communities, trade routes, the materials each landscape offered, and the historical period in which it flourished. This map follows fifteen of those traditions to the places where they took root.

      Geographic Spread of Indian Art

      Why does Warli painting look so different from a Kangra miniature, or Khajuraho from Thanjavur? Because each tradition answered to a particular place: who ruled it, what people believed, which communities lived there, what the land provided and who passed through. These nine forces explain most of the variety on the map.

      How influence travelled

      Traditions were distinct but not isolated. Artists moved when courts rose and fell, and later generations looked back to older sites. These four documented connections are drawn on the map when you switch on influence links.
